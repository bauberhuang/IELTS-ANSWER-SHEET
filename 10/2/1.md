This chart compares the destinations of UK graduates and postgraduates.

Overall, further study was the most common destination for both groups, followed by part-time work and unemployment, while voluntary work was chosen by very few people in either category. The main difference between the two groups was one of scale and proportion rather than ranking: graduate numbers were roughly ten times higher than postgraduate numbers overall, and the gap between further study and part-time work was considerably narrower among postgraduates than among graduates.

Further study was the leading destination in both groups, attracting approximately 30,000 graduates and 3,000 postgraduates. Part-time work was the second most common choice, accounting for 17,735 graduates and 2,535 postgraduates — around 59% of the further study figure among graduates, compared to roughly 85% among postgraduates, which shows that part-time work was almost as popular as further study for this group specifically.

Unemployment accounted for 16,235 graduates and 1,625 postgraduates, a similar proportion of just over half the further study total in both groups. Voluntary work was the least common destination by a clear margin, chosen by only 3,800 graduates and fewer than 500 postgraduates.
